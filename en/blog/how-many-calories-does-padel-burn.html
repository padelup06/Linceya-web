How Many Calories Does Padel Burn? Real Numbers

September 14, 2026 · ≈ 6 min read

If you’ve ever finished a padel match drenched in sweat and wondered what that actually cost you calorie-wise, you’re not alone. The short answer: most players burn between 400 and 600 calories per hour of padel, depending on intensity, weight, and how the match plays out.

That range is wide because padel isn’t one fixed effort level. A relaxed rally with friends feels nothing like a tight, competitive set where every point turns into a sprint. Here’s how to figure out where you actually fall — and how to burn more if that’s your goal.

Average Calories Burned Per Hour of Padel

For a person weighing around 70kg (154lbs), a moderate-intensity padel match burns roughly 450-500 calories per hour. That number climbs with body weight and drops slightly for lighter players, since calorie burn scales with mass.

Here’s a rough breakdown by weight, for one hour of moderately intense padel:

  • 60kg (132lbs): approximately 380-420 calories
  • 70kg (154lbs): approximately 450-500 calories
  • 80kg (176lbs): approximately 520-570 calories
  • 90kg (198lbs): approximately 590-650 calories

These numbers assume you’re actually moving — chasing balls, adjusting position, hitting with some pace. If you’re mostly standing near the net waiting for easy volleys, your burn will sit lower.

Padel sits in a similar calorie-burning category to tennis doubles or a brisk recreational squash game. It’s not as brutal as running intervals, but it’s far from a light stroll.

Why Intensity Changes Everything

Two people can play the exact same hour of padel and burn very different amounts of energy. The difference almost always comes down to intensity, not just time on court.

A casual, social match with lots of chatting between points might only get your heart rate into the fat-burning zone for half the session. A genuinely competitive match — long rallies, smashes, quick lateral movement — keeps your heart rate elevated almost continuously.

Match format matters too. Best-of-three sets with tiebreaks last longer and involve more total effort than a quick single set. If you’re tracking calories seriously, duration matters as much as intensity.

Skill level plays a role as well. Beginners often move less efficiently and cover more unnecessary ground, which can actually increase calorie burn even if their shots aren’t as clean. More advanced players move with better anticipation, which sometimes reduces wasted movement but increases point intensity through longer, faster rallies.

Padel vs Other Sports: How It Compares

Curious how padel calorie burn stacks up against other activities? Here’s a general comparison for one hour at moderate-to-vigorous intensity, based on a 70kg person:

  • Padel: 450-550 calories
  • Tennis (singles): 500-600 calories
  • Tennis (doubles): 350-450 calories
  • Running (moderate pace, 8km/h): 550-600 calories
  • Cycling (moderate pace): 450-500 calories
  • Swimming (moderate laps): 500-600 calories

Padel lands right in the middle of this list — comparable to cycling, slightly below running or tennis singles, and clearly above tennis doubles. That makes sense: padel’s smaller court means less total distance covered, but the fast reflexes and constant positioning keep effort levels high.

The real advantage padel has over many of these alternatives is enjoyment. Burning 500 calories on a treadmill feels like work. Burning the same amount during a fun match with friends barely feels like exercise at all — which matters enormously for long-term consistency.

How to Burn More Calories During a Padel Session

If your goal is maximizing calorie burn, a few adjustments make a real difference without changing the sport itself.

Play more aggressively. Chasing down balls, moving forward for volleys, and covering more court space all increase your total energy expenditure. Passive players who wait for the ball burn noticeably less.

Extend your rallies. Longer points mean more sustained effort. Focus on consistency instead of ending points early with risky shots — you’ll move more and burn more.

Play competitive matches, not casual hits. The intensity gap between a relaxed knock-around and a genuinely contested match is significant. If calorie burn matters to you, seek out evenly matched opponents.

Warm up properly and play multiple sets. A proper warm-up primes your body for higher output from the first point, and playing two or three sets instead of one obviously multiplies your total burn.

FAQ

How many calories does an hour of padel burn?

On average, an hour of padel burns between 400 and 600 calories, depending on your weight, intensity, and how competitive the match is. A relaxed social game sits closer to 350-450, while a fast, competitive match can push past 600 for heavier or fitter players.

Does padel burn more calories than tennis?

Tennis singles usually burns slightly more calories per hour than padel because the court is bigger and rallies involve more running. Padel doubles is gentler on total distance covered, but shorter court and quicker exchanges keep your heart rate high too.

Is padel good for weight loss?

Yes. Padel combines cardio and short bursts of intense effort, which helps burn fat and build endurance over time. Playing two to three times a week, combined with a balanced diet, can support steady weight loss while being genuinely fun to stick with.

Does playing padel doubles burn fewer calories than singles?

Padel is almost always played as doubles, so there’s no true singles comparison like in tennis. That said, more aggressive doubles pairs who cover the court actively burn noticeably more than passive players who stay put near the net.

What factors affect how many calories I burn playing padel?

Your body weight, match intensity, skill level, and how much you move all matter. Heavier players burn more in absolute terms. Competitive matches with long rallies burn more than casual games. Better footwork and court coverage also increase your total burn.
